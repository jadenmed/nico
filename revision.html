Explanation of Revisions

For my Revised Major Project, I chose my Annotated Bibliography, the source-gathering foundation for everything else I wrote this semester. My first draft used first-person language throughout, including inside individual source annotations, and referred broadly to "young people" instead of naming the specific population my research actually covers — a phrase my instructor flagged directly as too vague. It also never stated a clear thesis, and every source link in my reference list appeared as plain, unclickable text.

These were global changes rather than sentence-level edits, since fixing the voice, the population, and the argument each required re-examining how every section of the document depended on the others, not just correcting isolated lines:

  • Rewrote the entire document in consistent third-person academic voice. The first-person language that ran through the original draft, including inside individual source annotations, was replaced throughout with consistent third-person academic voice.
  • Replaced every vague reference with the specific population. "Young people" became college students ages 18 to 29 — the specific population that my six sources actually study — everywhere the term appeared in the document.
  • Added a standalone thesis statement. The original draft never stated a clear thesis. The revision adds a standalone thesis statement summarizing my central argument up front.
  • Reformatted the reference list as live hyperlinks. Every citation in the original reference list appeared as plain, unclickable text. In the revision, every reference is a live, working hyperlink.
